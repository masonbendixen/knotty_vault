## TL;DR — the strategic bet

You have two very different revenue streams available, and they're better together than apart:

1. **Software consulting** — high leverage ($/hour), but you've never sold it and the sales cycle is longer. Your background (MS / Google / Meta / Tableau, Spanner, Fuchsia, SQL Server, .NET interop, VR) is *elite and scarce*. That's your edge — lean into the hard stuff, not the commodity stuff.
2. **Massage** — immediate local cash, high hourly, physically taxing and time-bound (doesn't scale). Best used as fast cash **and** a funnel into both the studio and consulting.

**My recommendation:** position consulting around your *scarce* skills (deep systems, databases, and AI-accelerated engineering — the last is your single most timely play), get fast cash from **expert networks + your warm network + vetted marketplaces**, and run massage as immediate income that also seeds your local client base for the studio. **Do not** position yourself as a generic "I build websites" freelancer — that's the one place you're badly underselling a 30-year systems career (more on that below).

The biggest near-term unlock is almost certainly **reaching out to people who already know you**. Thirty years means a large network of ex-colleagues now in founder/VP/CTO roles. That is, by a wide margin, the highest-conversion channel a first-time consultant has.

## 1. Reframing: you're not a "website guy"

Website-building is a brutal, commoditized race-to-the-bottom (Wix/Squarespace/Fiverr/overseas agencies). Charging what your time is worth there is nearly impossible, and it uses ~5% of your skill set. Two reframes:

- **For brochure sites:** don't compete. Refer those people to Squarespace and keep your time. Only take web work when it's *real software* — custom booking/payments/portals/integrations — which you can price like engineering, not like a $1,500 site.
- **Your eCommerce portal is the proof.** The Knotty Yoga platform (C++/Crow + Angular + Postgres, Square payments, multi-tenant, scheduling/series/refunds) is a serious full-stack + payments + architecture case study. Most "web consultants" can't build what you already built. Write it up as a one-page case study and let it do the talking.

Sell the *hard problem*, not the *deliverable category*.

## 3. The AI-accelerated engineering angle (develop this — it's your strongest, most timely play)

Almost every eng org in 2026 is flailing at "how do we actually get value from AI coding tools." You have a rare combo: senior credibility **and** a real, working methodology (spec-driven development in Obsidian, Claude Code, structured planning docs — exactly what you're doing on this project). Productize it:

- **"AI-accelerated delivery" audit** (fixed, 1–2 weeks): assess a team's current workflow, tooling, guardrails, and ship a concrete playbook + ROI estimate.
- **Team enablement workshop** (½–2 days, fixed fee): hands-on — spec-driven planning, prompt/context discipline, review workflows, where AI helps vs. hurts, guardrails for correctness. You can run this remote.
- **Done-with-you pilot** (2–4 weeks): pick one real feature, ship it alongside their team using your methodology, leave them the repeatable process. This converts skeptics and often turns into a retainer.
- **Productivity multiplier on your *own* contract work.** Quote outcomes/fixed scope rather than hours — your AI-augmented throughput becomes margin instead of being competed away.

Proof assets that sell this: a short case study of *this* eCommerce build ("X features in Y weeks, solo, AI-augmented"), and a couple of LinkedIn posts showing the actual workflow. "Build in public" is the cheapest top-of-funnel you have.

## 4. Massage — fast cash *and* a funnel, not just a side gig

- **Immediate, local, high hourly** — the quickest money while the studio comes together.
- **B2B chair massage** is the clever version: tech companies and events pay corporate rates for on-site chair massage, it's recurring, and it physically puts you in the room with the exact people who buy consulting. Two birds.
- **Studio synergy:** every massage client pre-launch is a warm lead for the studio's opening. Capture name/email (consistent with how the studio handles contacts) and you're building the launch list now.
- **Caution:** massage trades time for money and is physically tiring — and that fatigue competes directly with studio build-out energy. Cap the hours (see open questions) and treat it as the *floor* of your income, not the ceiling. Confirm licensure + liability insurance are current before taking B2B work.

## 5. Where to actually find work (you asked specifically)

Ranked roughly by speed-to-cash for *your* profile:

1. **Your warm network (do this first, this week).** A short, specific message to ex-MS/Google/Meta/Tableau colleagues and anyone who's now a founder/VP/CTO: "I'm taking on a limited number of consulting clients while I get my studio off the ground. I help with [systems/databases/AI-accelerated delivery]. Know anyone who could use that?" Referrals from people who've seen you work convert far better than any cold channel.
2. **Expert networks** — GLG, AlphaSights, Guidepoint, Coleman, Third Bridge, Dialectica, Tegus. Free to join, they match you to paid client calls based on your background. Near-zero sales effort, fits around studio prep, pays $300–1,000/hr prorated. Your résumé is exactly what they want. Sign up to several.
3. **Vetted senior marketplaces** — **Toptal** and **A.Team** are the best fits (they handle sales/matching and take a cut); also Braintrust (no talent fee), Gun.io, Arc.dev. **Bolster** and **Catalant** for fractional-exec / advisory / board roles.
4. **Technical due diligence** — reach out directly to any VC/PE contacts; many keep a bench of freelance technical experts for diligence. High rate, episodic, intellectually fun.
5. **Local / SMB** — chamber of commerce, BNI, local startup meetups, the studio's own community. This is where the (elevated, custom-build) "website/software" work lives. Also where chair-massage contracts come from.
6. **Inbound over time** — a simple services one-pager + an active LinkedIn presence + occasional posts on AI-accelerated engineering. Slower, compounding.

## 7. Pricing & first-timer setup

- **Entity:** single-member LLC + a separate business bank account (you may already have one for the studio; a separate consulting entity or at least separate books keeps massage / studio / consulting clean). Talk to your accountant about whether one LLC or separate ones is better given the studio.
- **Insurance:** E&O / professional liability for consulting; confirm massage liability coverage for B2B.
- **Contracts:** a simple **MSA + per-engagement SOW**. Get one lawyer review of a reusable template, then reuse it. Watch these clauses: IP assignment scope, **limitation of liability** (cap at fees paid), kill fee / termination-for-convenience, net-15/30 payment, deposit up front, change-of-scope handling.
- **Rates:** anchor high and hold. For your profile $150–250/hr is *conservative* for general work, $250–400 for diligence/specialized, $300–800+ for expert calls. Move to **fixed/weekly/value pricing** as soon as you're comfortable — it rewards your AI-augmented speed instead of penalizing it. The first-timer instinct to "be cheap to win it" is the main mistake to avoid.
- **Deposit + milestones** on fixed projects; don't float a stranger's build.
- **Money hygiene:** invoicing/time tool (Harvest, Bonsai, or Wave-free), set aside ~30% for taxes, pay quarterly estimates, track expenses (home office, tools, insurance, mileage).

## 9. Pitfalls for a first-time consultant

- **Underpricing** to feel "safe" — it's the #1 mistake and it's hard to raise later.
- **Scope creep** with no SOW or change process — bleeds your studio time away for free.
- **Trading all your hours** (massage + hourly consulting) so nothing scales — keep at least one *leveraged* offering (fixed-price productized work, retainer, or your own IP).
- **Letting consulting eat the studio.** The studio is the long-term asset; consulting is the bridge. Time-box it.
- **Building your own marketing site instead of doing outreach.** Outreach pays; polishing your site doesn't.
- **One-client dependence** — don't let a single engagement become 100% of income with no pipeline.

## 10. Wilder / longer-horizon ideas

There may be a small SaaS or a "starter platform" template business hiding in your own codebase — and you'd be your own first reference customer.

- **Open-source a slice** of your tooling/methodology for visibility → inbound.
- **Advisory/board seats** for early studios-tech or dev-tools startups via Bolster.
